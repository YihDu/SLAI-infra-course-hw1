## Implemented ideas

1. `d1_schedule.load_ahead`: issue the next double-buffered weight transfer
   before the current MMA. The original sequence let MMA operand/accumulator
   dependencies block the subsequent load on the in-order workgroup tape.
   Same arithmetic and RF buffers; scheduler protects buffer reuse.
2. `d1_schedule.fuse_gelu`: finish bias and tanh GELU in W1's accumulator,
   using dead operand buffers RF1/2, then store the activated result directly.
   Remove the intermediate W1 HBM round trip and standalone GELU phase.
   Persistent RF weights in lanes 5+ remain untouched.

Both features default off. Frozen `check_case` has passed seeds 7 and 19,
including HBM race validation. Frozen timing matches the 75434-cycle
development prediction exactly, area is 23.5301 mm² and D1 peak rolling power
is 10.5005 W. Joint grade remains pending.
`refine.py` generates isolated snapshots, checks two seeds, measures with the

`verify.py` uses the unmodified frozen `check_case` and `estimate_case`.
Regenerating both programs with the staged recipe produces byte-identical

## What a further 5x actually requires

Score is proportional to the inverse square root of the product of the two
latencies. A further 5x requires a **25x smaller latency product**, not a 5x
reduction in either case alone. Relative to verified cand-148, the target is
about 104106 points, or P1*D1 <= 3.724 billion cycles squared. For example,
P1=186000 and D1=20000 would nearly meet that target. This is a target, not an
established achievable bound.

The 16-SM P1 trace (`../p1-fuse/v6_16sm/critpath-4ch.json`) attributes:

- HBM->RF: 255366 cycles (45.3% of critical path).
- MMA: 187782 cycles (33.3%).
- Prompt W1/W2 together: 255529 cycles (45.3%).
- Merged new-position W1/W2: 81695 cycles (14.5%).
- HBM reads: 32.67 MB; total energy: 7.205 mJ; peak window: 10.89 W.

At unchanged total energy, even the loose whole-run 20 W energy bound is
180130 P1 cycles at 500 MHz. Fivefold P1 speedup from 563776 would require
112755 cycles, so it necessarily needs less energy as well as better overlap.
This bound is necessary only, not sufficient for the rolling-window limit.

Priorities after the current frozen checks:

1. P1 weight reuse across the two prompt batches and the generated-position
   phase, with explicit SH ownership/lifetimes. The 16 SMs currently split
   batch/team work; duplicated weight traffic and step reloads survive.
   Redesign as row/column partition with measured broadcast/cache behavior.
2. P1 MLP dataflow: retain W1 activation panels for W2 consumption; avoid
   writing and redistributing the whole intermediate. Quantify extra partial
   reductions against saved RF/HBM traffic before implementing.
3. D1 per-layer workgroup/RF lifetime redesign: current persistent weights
   share one 64 KiB slot across both layers and reserve scratch lanes, forcing
   many weights through SH->RF on every step. Separate persistent layer
   ownership could use more of each SM's 256 KiB RF, but adds synchronization
   and shares bandwidth. Measure it; do not assume extra WGs add RF bandwidth.

Do not reopen blind tile sweeps. New structures should have explicit capacity,
read/write traffic, synchronization and instruction-count estimates. The
remaining 5x is a research direction, not supported by current measurements.
